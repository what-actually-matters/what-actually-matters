THE WAM  5
Five themes, drawn from eleven assessed touchpoints, that will move the needle most. Each one ranges from a fast, free fix to a genuine investment. This is not a ranked order. Every theme below matters.
01Build a Commercial Engine, Not Just a Coffee Counter
The Finding
Revenue Generation scored 1.1 out of 10, the lowest score anywhere in this report, and Ordering Experience scored only 3.5 for the same underlying reason. No upsell, no cross-sell, no loyalty mention, no retail sales, across an entire visit, on a product good enough to easily support all four. This is not a missed moment to coach away. It's an entire revenue channel that doesn't currently exist in this business.
THINK
Quick Wins
  • Give every barista one confident pairing suggestion to offer with the best-selling drink, so the ask is consistent rather than improvised.
  • Put the loyalty programme physically into the transaction, a QR code pointed to verbally at the till, not just signage on a wall.
  • Move the retail bean bags beside the till, within arm's reach, with one line staff can use when handing over a finished order.
Bigger Plays
  • Invest in a proper point-of-sale and loyalty platform that prompts upsells automatically and captures guest data for personalised offers, rather than relying on staff memory alone.
  • Create a part-time guest experience lead role, responsible for coaching commercial behaviour into the team on an ongoing basis, not a one-off briefing that fades within a month.
  • Redesign the counter as a genuine merchandising zone, with dedicated fixtures and lighting, treating retail as a real second business line rather than an afterthought on a low shelf.

02Engineer Real Capacity for Peak Demand
The Finding
Speed of Service scored 5.8 out of 10, with every stage measured running over target, and seating availability scored just 4 out of 10 at the same hour. These are not two separate problems. They are the same morning rush hitting two different parts of the business at once, a single till and a single barista absorbing demand the layout and staffing were never built to handle.
THINK
Quick Wins
  • Open the second till automatically once the queue reaches three or more guests, rather than leaving it to individual judgement.
  • Add peak-hours-only seating signage to protect tables for ordering guests during the busiest window of the day.
Bigger Plays
  • Invest in a second espresso machine and grinder station, enabling two baristas to genuinely work in parallel rather than sharing one physical bottleneck.
  • Reconfigure the counter layout to create a true express lane for simple orders, separated physically from the full bar queue, not just by a verbal policy.
  • Bring on a dedicated peak-hours team member, rather than stretching the existing rota thinner during the two busiest hours of the day.

03Turn Product Quality Into a Genuine Brand Story
The Finding
Product Quality scored 8.6 out of 10, among the strongest results in this report, while bean origin knowledge scored just 6 and Brand Standards landed at a modest 6.8, held back specifically by merchandising tucked out of sight. The coffee is doing all the work alone. Nothing currently built around it, in staff knowledge or in the physical brand presentation, is reinforcing why it's worth what's being charged.
THINK
Quick Wins
  • Keep a one-page origin and tasting card behind the counter, updated whenever the bean changes, so the answer is always confident rather than improvised.
  • Encourage one unprompted product detail per order, not only when asked, since most guests won't think to ask unless invited into the story first.
Bigger Plays
  • Invest in formal barista training or certification, turning product knowledge into a genuine, structured skill rather than something picked up informally on shift.
  • Commission proper branded merchandising and in-store signage, designed to match the quality of the product rather than a low shelf and a faded poster.
  • Build a direct, storytelling relationship with the roaster or origin farm, with real content the brand can use in-store and online, not generic origin facts.

04Make Every Guest Relationship Outlast the Visit
The Finding
Guest Recovery scored a respectable 7.2, with the mistake owned and fixed within 90 seconds at no charge, but the follow-up afterwards scored just 4. Staff Behaviour told a similar story at 7.8 overall, strong on warmth but only 6 on proactive assistance and ownership. In both cases, the team does the right thing when prompted. The relationship-building moment that comes after, the part a guest actually remembers, isn't being built deliberately.
THINK
Quick Wins
  • Build a simple two-step recovery standard: fix the issue fast, then return within a minute or two to confirm it landed correctly.
  • Give staff one small, specific gesture to offer alongside a genuine mistake, turning an error into a memorable positive rather than a neutral correction.
Bigger Plays
  • Invest in a genuine guest recognition system, so regulars are actually remembered and acknowledged, not just tracked anonymously through a generic loyalty app.
  • Run a structured proactive-service training programme across the whole team, shifting the culture from responding well when asked to anticipating before being asked.

05Make Excellence Repeatable Across Every Shift
The Finding
Greeting scored a strong 7.5 overall, but queue management at the greeting point scored just 5, with guests further back left unacknowledged during the rush. The warmth this team shows is real. What isn't yet built is a system that guarantees it survives a busy morning, a new hire's first week, or a shift when the usual strongest staff member isn't on the floor.
THINK
Quick Wins
  • Acknowledge guests further back in the queue with a glance or a nod, not only the guest currently being served.
  • Use the strongest greeting observed in this visit as the internal benchmark in the next team briefing, showing rather than describing what good looks like.
Bigger Plays
  • Build a formal onboarding curriculum for new hires, so warmth and consistency don't depend on which specific staff happen to be on shift that day.
  • Introduce a shift-lead structure with clear accountability for standards, rather than quality depending on whoever happens to be working.
This is what a genuine evaluation looks like. Eleven touchpoints, from the moment the shop comes into view to the final taste of the coffee. Specific, evidenced findings, not vague impressions, converted into five themes that each carry a fast fix and a real investment, not just one or the other.
